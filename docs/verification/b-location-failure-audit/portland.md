**Why a complete nominal assignment is insufficient.**

The positive certificate in `maritime/search/gpu/resident/engine.py` lines 302–324 requires every report to have a nominal group assignment, each selected group to be exact, every selected representative residual to lie strictly inside the physical bearing tolerance after the numerical margin, zero candidate flags, known-water position, and the required world certificates. Group angular extent can enlarge the possible nominal match support, but lines 308–311 explicitly prohibit using that extent as a physical displacement of the representative. Therefore 67 nominal group matches do not by themselves certify 67 physically bounded representative bearings.

The zero-flag requirement is already sufficient to explain the absence of positive certification in Portland because every terminal center has flag 8. Other positive-certificate conditions may also fail, but the terminal archive does not preserve selected per-report residuals, group exactness, or point states to isolate them. Consequently the evidence does not establish that flag 8 was the only failing condition.

`tools/select_resident_result.py` lines 51–55 separately require valid, distinct assignment IDs, finite cost, status 0, and flags 0. Lines 57–77 select the minimum summed absolute residual, then east and north for ties. Lines 93–94 intentionally save the certified estimate separately from the best complete nominal assignment. Portland's `estimate.json` lines 26–35 accordingly save `estimate` as null and a diagnostic complete assignment with status 2, flag 8, and 67 matched reports. The selector did not overlook a certified candidate.

**Why an unrelated numerical boundary can prevent certification.**

Flag 8 is candidate-wide. In `maritime/search/gpu/resident/parallel_prediction.cu` lines 48–53, it marks near-zero range or a range-limit boundary. Lines 62–64 OR every gathered pair's flags into the candidate before rejecting unaccepted pairs. A warning can therefore remain even when that pair is not retained as a visible prediction. In `parallel_prediction.py` lines 194–197, any visible bearing gap close to the 0.1-degree grouping threshold sets the candidate flag. Lines 248–254 set it for any raw group's near-tied nearest representatives before vessel-represented groups are removed. Finally, `engine.py` lines 256–265 set it for any report-by-group gate edge near its numerical decision boundary, including edges that are not selected by the assignment. The positive-certification test in lines 312–314 consumes that global flag.

Portland's simulation diagnostics contain concrete near-coincident catalog records that can produce the nearest-range-tie warning. The group at observation 20 contains `enc-component:approach:Approach.Light_Float_point:US4ME1BD.000:approach.litflt.fid=16423` at latitude 43.711988900000506 and longitude -70.18813330000012, plus `chart-coordinate:0x1.5db2273c84eb8p+5:-0x1.18c0a6040b278p+6` at latitude 43.711988899999994 and longitude -70.1881333. Groups at observations 24 and 26 contain similarly near-coincident component and coordinate records. They retain separate physical IDs in the saved diagnostics and have no identity-limited members. The tie rule counts group members within 0.002 m of the minimum range in `parallel_prediction.cu` lines 114–124, then flags more than one tie in `parallel_prediction.py` line 249. These static catalog facts identify a specific plausible boundary source. They do not establish which branch actually set the flag at each terminal center, because only the aggregated bit was saved. No score or visibility calculation was repeated to resolve that missing provenance.

**Physical rejection and diagnostic accuracy.**

The independent physical graph is distinct from nominal grouping. `maritime/search/gpu/resident/distinct_pruning.py` lines 1–8 describe an overapproximation of individual physical report origins, excluding vessels and carrying no negative evidence from missing detections. Lines 203–211 distinguish possible, proved-impossible, and unsupported graph results. `optimized.py` lines 87–97 applies that proof to zero-size cells at the sampled centers. The status priority in `engine.py` lines 27–35 and 340–349 permits an independent physical contradiction to establish inconsistency despite nominal warnings. Portland's 23 reason-2 centers lack a possible object for at least one positive report. Its seven reason-4 centers lack an injective explanation. Those 30 centers have actual recorded physical-positive rejection; the remaining 60 do not have a saved rejection certificate. Reason 0 alone does not prove physical feasibility because unsupported graph cases also retain the center.

The best saved complete nominal assignment is at latitude 43.66501233780062 and longitude -70.22499067006197, with summed absolute residual 8.452304990244073 degrees. `truth.json` saves latitude 43.665 and longitude -70.225. A WGS84 inverse-geodesic comparison of these existing coordinates gives 1.5637690135940512 m. Matching the manifest's bearing order to the saved simulator diagnostics shows that this assignment uses the generating representative for 57 reports and exchanges the representative identities within five close-bearing pairs for the other ten. Anonymous association permits these exchanges. They do not demonstrate false aid reports or a simulator/search catalog mismatch.

The observed outcome is therefore a conservative certification failure around an accurately localized nominal solution. The 67 aid reports strongly restrict the sampled neighborhood, but the candidate-wide numerical boundary rule prevents a certified estimate there. No land center or uncertain-geometry flag is present in the saved terminal output. The available evidence does not implicate an uncataloged physical aid, map mismatch, or missed coast-wide search region. It also cannot identify the exact warning origin or prove that every other positive-certificate condition would pass if that warning were resolved.
