Conservative Coast-Wide Localization from
Unassociated Global Bearings to Charted Aids
J.C. Vaught

Abstract—Bearing-only localization against a coastal chart
requires simultaneous position search and association of observations with physical navigation aids. This paper presents a
deterministic hierarchical method that covers a declared coastal
domain while rejecting only regions with a supported impossibility proof. A true-north bearing enclosure accounts for observercell extent, chart-position uncertainty, and numerical margins. A
bipartite graph tests whether every trusted aid report can receive
a distinct possible chart object. Missing aids incur no penalty, and
vessel detections create no chart-aid obligation. Spatial indexes,
reusable prepared geometry, device-resident subdivision, parallel
visibility prediction, and cooperative assignment concentrate
computation on surviving regions. A recorded Eastern Seaboard
search for one corrected simulated Charleston frame evaluated
64,875 regions from 39,435 initial cells in 8.462 s on three
graphics processing units. The selected terminal center matched
all 64 aid reports and lay 2.060 m from independently evaluated
truth. The traversal retained 397 terminal cells, including 182
unresolved centers near the selected estimate. These results
demonstrate rapid complete-domain traversal for this frame
while distinguishing whole-cell exclusion, pointwise consistency,
and unresolved local ambiguity.
Index Terms—Bearing-only localization, chart aids, bounded
error, data association, conservative pruning, parallel search.

I. I NTRODUCTION
A coastal observer can measure directions to navigation aids
without measuring their ranges or recognizing their individual
chart identities. When its heading is known relative to true
north, each direction constrains position directly in a global
angular frame. However, a large chart contains many alternative landmark arrangements. A plausible local angular fit
therefore does not establish that the observer is in the correct
harbor. The search must account for the complete declared
domain while treating unavailable detections and incomplete
visibility conservatively.
This problem combines geometric resection with combinatorial association. Bearings to identified landmarks already
support robot localization [1], but chart objects must first be
assigned to the observed directions here. Independent nearestangle assignments can reuse one aid for multiple detections
and create a false explanation. Meanwhile, rejecting a location
because an expected aid was not detected would impose
an additional detection-completeness assumption. The method
instead treats observed aids as positive obligations and permits
unobserved catalog objects.
The central computational choice is to test regions before
scoring positions. Large rectangles are discarded when no position inside can explain the positive measurements. Surviving
rectangles are subdivided, causing geometric uncertainty to

contract and enabling stronger proofs at finer scales. Expensive
shoreline prediction and exact nominal assignment are then
concentrated on the small terminal population. Thus, the coastwide domain is covered by a hierarchy of region decisions
rather than by an exhaustive fine grid.
This paper explains the resulting bounded-error model, safe
region tests, distinct-object association, and parallel execution
strategy. It then analyzes the existing corrected Charleston
record [2]. The evaluation uses archived search outputs, timing
records, and an independently frozen estimate.
II. R ELATED G EOMETRIC AND A SSOCIATION M ETHODS
Bearing resection and set-membership estimation supply
complementary precedents. Betke and Gurvits estimate position and orientation using bearings to landmarks with known
identities [1]. Colle and Galerne formulate multiangulation
through bounded-error set inversion [3]. The present formulation adopts a feasible-set interpretation but includes unknown correspondence, chart completeness, and land occlusion. Known global heading removes orientation from the
search state and leaves a two-dimensional position domain.
Association must also be tested jointly. Neira and Tardós
show that individually compatible matches need not form
a jointly compatible assignment in stochastic mapping [4].
Here, compatibility is deterministic and interval-based rather
than covariance-based. Its central combinatorial condition is
injectivity. Hall’s representative theorem characterizes when
every report can receive a distinct neighboring object [5].
Maximum bipartite matching tests this requirement without
enumerating all permutations.
Global geometric search also benefits from bounds over
regions. Go-ICP combines branch-and-bound with geometric
error bounds for globally optimal rigid point-set registration
[6]. The present search uses subdivision and impossibility
bounds, but does not use an incumbent residual to discard
all inferior regions or certify a continuous global residual
optimum. It retains every region not disproved at the stopping
scale. This distinction separates exhaustive domain coverage
from optimality of the selected sampled center.
Finally, the coastal scale requires a consistent geographic
frame. Ellipsoidal inverse geodesics provide distance and
forward azimuth between observer and chart objects, with
differential quantities that support sensitivity reasoning [7].
The angular enclosure below is a derived bound used by
this method. It is not an uncertainty guarantee stated by that
reference for the particular numerical implementation.

B. Physical Identity and Finite Angular Resolution
A source chart can describe one physical installation
through separate light, beacon, daymark, or topmark records.
Counting those components as separate objects would corrupt both simulation and injective association. A common
canonicalization rule is therefore applied before simulation
and search. The added consolidation step merges records with
exactly equal parsed latitude and longitude, across source
chart names and component labels. It complements existing
physical-component links in the chart importer. Nearby unequal coordinates remain distinct under this added rule.
This common physical catalog prevents a simulator/search
mismatch in the number of possible aids. Merged objects
retain component provenance and conservative position and

extent information. Conflicting geometry disables an unsupported exact-position assertion. Physical identity and position
certainty remain separate properties. A stable identity does not
establish that the coordinate is exact.
Finite angular resolution introduces a second grouping operation. At a candidate position, visible objects that cannot be
angularly separated are represented by circular groups and a
frontmost representative. Physical catalog consolidation occurs
before this view-dependent grouping. A broad group extent
can permit a possible explanation, but cannot certify that its
representative bearing may move freely throughout that extent.
Positive terminal certification therefore checks the selected
representative within the physical bearing tolerance.
C. Independent Chart and Shoreline Support
The map supplies known land, known water, and unknown
regions. A point on known land cannot be the observer. A
clear sight line is required to certify a particular visible aid
explanation. Unknown geometry remains unresolved when it
can affect that explanation.
Chart completeness and shoreline coverage support different
conclusions. A complete chart catalog can prove that no aid
exists in the relevant range-and-bearing envelope even when
shoreline coverage is unknown. Unknown land may hide a
catalog object, but it cannot create an absent chart object.
Consequently, an absence proof needs complete chart-object
support over the full cell visibility envelope, while a land proof
needs reliable shoreline geometry. Keeping these premises
separate avoids disabling useful chart proofs merely because
an unrelated area of shoreline is unknown.
IV. C ONSERVATIVE H IERARCHICAL S EARCH
A. Covering the Domain
The search initially covers D with rectangular root cells.
Each surviving nonterminal cell is divided into four children
by midpoint splits. A terminal cell has maximum side length
no greater than the declared threshold. Every root must be
processed, and every survivor must reach that stopping scale.
There is no fixed top-ranked harbor shortlist or top-N candidate cutoff.
For a rectangle C, a pruning decision must establish that
every observer position in C is impossible under the accepted
premises. A failed center fit is insufficient because a different
point inside the same rectangle may fit. This yields a coverage
invariant. At every stage, D is covered by previously rejected
cells together with the retained frontier and completed terminal
cells. Unsupported geometry, unavailable certificates, numerical uncertainty, or exhausted graph capacity cannot remove
coverage.
The hierarchy avoids generating the complete fine grid. With
initial width s0 and terminal threshold st , a full quadtree would
contain approximately n0 4L leaves, where L = ⌈log2 (s0 /st )⌉
and n0 is the root count. For s0 = 10 km, st = 1 m,
and n0 = 39,435, L = 14 and the hypothetical full leaf
population is approximately 1.059 × 1013 . This is a geometric
calculation, not a measured baseline. The recorded traversal

instead reaches 397 terminal cells because most ancestors are
rejected.

C. Injective Explanation of Positive Reports
For each cell, construct a bipartite graph GC = (I, JC , EC )
between trusted reports and possible physical chart objects. An
edge (i, j) exists whenever object j can possibly explain report
i somewhere in C under the range, bearing, extent, positionsupport, and available whole-cell visibility bounds. Known
vessels cannot create edges, while objects with unknown type
remain possible aids. An edge is an outer approximation
of possibility. Different edges may require different observer
positions, so an allowed graph is not itself a feasible location
certificate.
Cheap necessary tests first reject a cell when any report has
no neighbor or when fewer than |I| distinct possible objects
exist. These tests alone allow several reports to rely on the
same smaller subset. The stronger test seeks a matching that
saturates all reports. Hall’s condition states
|NC (S)| ≥ |S|

where NC (S) is the set of neighboring objects [5]. An
augmenting-path matching algorithm tests saturation without
explicitly enumerating the subsets.
The rejection implication is direct. If a valid location existed
in C, its distinct physical explanations would form a saturating
matching in the outer graph. Therefore, absence of such a
matching proves that no valid location exists in the entire cell.
Finding a matching merely prevents this rejection. It does not
show that all matched edges are simultaneously realizable at
one observer position. The saved graph evidence includes a
failure with three reports, two neighboring objects, and six
edges. Every report has an individual explanation, yet the three
reports cannot receive distinct objects.
D. Land Occlusion and Terminal Certification
Whole-cell land rejection requires strict containment in
known land. For aid occlusion, a certified barrier can remove
an edge only when it blocks sight lines for the entire observer
cell and supported target position. Unknown or boundarydependent obstruction retains the edge. Such sufficient barrier
witnesses need not detect every actual occlusion.
After subdivision, each terminal center receives a more
detailed nominal prediction. Ellipsoidal bearings, range, polygon shoreline sight lines, circular grouping, and frontmost
representatives define a candidate’s visible groups. A gated
injective assignment maximizes matched-report cardinality
and minimizes the sum of absolute angular residuals among
assignments with that cardinality. The Hungarian assignment
formulation provides the exact finite assignment mechanism
[8]. Unobserved groups incur no penalty.
A center is consistent only when every positive report has a
certified selected explanation and required geometry and numerical checks pass. A certified physical impossibility makes
the center inconsistent. A nominal grouped assignment failure
remains unresolved when the conservative physical graph still
permits an explanation. Numerical warnings and unknown
visibility also prevent unsupported acceptance. These center

labels do not change the coverage status of the surrounding
terminal rectangle.
The selected output is the consistent terminal center with
minimum summed absolute residual, using deterministic eastthen-north ties. This ranks sampled supported explanations.
It neither proves a unique association nor minimizes residual
over every continuous position in every remaining cell. Truth is
excluded from selection and evaluated only after the estimate
is frozen.
V. W HY C OAST-W IDE S EARCH I S C OMPUTATIONALLY
P RACTICAL
The hierarchy reduces total work, while indexing reduces
the cost of each remaining decision. Balanced spatial trees
over chart objects and shoreline geometry are prepared once. A
cell query gathers only objects in its expanded range envelope,
and visibility queries traverse relevant geometry rather than
repeatedly scanning the full map. The range and bearing
graph is built before full nominal grouped prediction, so
rejected cells avoid both descendant generation and terminal
assignment.
Prepared geometry is reusable across measurement frames
and stopping resolutions. A content-bound cache stores projected arrays and spatial indexes. Its identity includes map and
catalog content, preparation options, and relevant geometry
preparation information. Measurements and terminal resolution do not define the static world. Physical-object consolidation uses a separately keyed object view that reuses shoreline
arrays while rebuilding only the affected object index. This
separation permits catalog-identity correction without reconstructing the map. Reuse still requires input validation, reading
cached arrays, and transferring them to a new device process.
Within a search, every selected graphics processing unit
(GPU) holds its own prepared arrays and evolving cell batches.
Independent root tasks are scheduled across devices. Cell
generation, region pruning, compaction, subdivision, bearing
prediction, visibility calculation, and association remain on the
devices during traversal. The central processing unit (CPU)
prepares inputs, launches work, schedules root tasks, synchronizes counters, and writes outputs. Device residence therefore
reduces repeated host transfer without implying zero host
activity.
There are two further levels of parallelism. Candidate/object
pairs distribute geodesic and visibility work across threads.
Stable sorting and grouped reductions preserve circular grouping and representative selection. Within each finite assignment,
cooperative column scans and potential updates share work
across a warp. All-forbidden columns are compacted before
assignment and mapped back afterward. Successive augmentations retain sequential dependence, and spatial gathering
retains per-candidate work. These dependencies explain why
moving arithmetic onto a GPU is insufficient by itself to
guarantee efficient execution.
The distinct-object graph also changes the amount of
work rather than merely accelerating an unchanged kernel.
A stronger cell test may cost more locally but eliminate

many descendants and nominal assignments. Its value must
therefore be assessed using both region counts and completedsearch time. The recorded comparison below measures their
combined effect for the revised pruning rules, including independent chart support and distinct-marker reasoning. It is not
an isolated ablation of matching alone.
VI. R ECORDED C HARLESTON E VALUATION
A. Inputs, Timing, and Coverage
The evaluated frame simulates an observer at 32.770000◦
north, 79.905000◦ west. It contains 70 reports, comprising 64
physical aids and six vessels. A 10 km sensor range is used.
The search covers the original Eastern Seaboard rectangle,
with east span 1,644.676 km and north span 2,388.459 km.
It begins with a 165 × 239 array of 39,435 cells of nominal
10 km width. Edge cells can be clipped by the domain. The
terminal threshold is 1 m, producing a nominal interior dyadic
width of 10,000/214 = 0.61035 m. This geometric resolution
is distinct from estimation accuracy.
The chart source is the National Oceanic and Atmospheric
Administration Electronic Navigational Chart (NOAA ENC)
Direct GIS extract, combined with an ENC shoreline mosaic.
The canonical world contains 32,168 chart objects and 2,268
vessel records, for 34,436 objects in total. Its prepared land
layer contains 11,274,092 boundary segments, with separate
coverage and unknown-region layers. The sensor angular resolution is 0.1◦ .
The search uses three NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada Generation
GPUs, each with approximately 48 GB memory, on an Intel
Xeon w9-3495X host with 56 physical cores. Geometry,
bearings, and assignment costs use 64-bit floating-point arithmetic. Recorded batches contain up to 512 cells, root tasks
contain up to eight roots, and nearby-object capacity is 2,048
per candidate. A 2 GB per-device workspace budget bounds
temporary allocation. Capacity exhaustion retains uncertainty
rather than truncating possible objects.
The saved updated search completed in 8.461936 s on
devices 0–2. Every root was completed exactly once across
4,930 scheduled tasks. Independent saved-output verification
counted 13,899, 13,552, and 11,984 roots on the respective
devices. Search time includes device setup, coordination, synchronization, and output work inside the search operation. It
excludes the separate input/cache-loading phase.
The complete recorded pipeline took 23.449 s, including
7.388 s of input/cache loading, selection from saved outputs,
separate truth evaluation, and cache-preservation checks. Both
caches were loaded without rebuilding, and their hashes,
sizes, and modification times matched before and after. Consequently, 8.462 s describes this search stage with reusable
prepared inputs, while 23.449 s describes the wider recorded
pipeline [2].

VII. D ISCUSSION AND C ONCLUSION
The observed speed follows from three linked choices.
First, complete-domain subdivision makes coarse impossibility
proofs eliminate descendants. Second, true-bearing intervals
and simultaneous distinct-object matching make those proofs
selective enough to remove nearly all coarse coastal alternatives. Third, static indexing and device-resident parallel
arithmetic reduce the repeated cost of evaluating the remaining
cells. The recorded comparison supports their combined effect
while preserving the previous consistent solution set.
The guarantees remain conditional on the model. Trusted aid
detections must represent true distinct physical objects, truenorth heading must be supplied, angular errors must respect
the hard bound, and chart identity and position support must
match the asserted catalog. Shoreline coverage and numerical
bounds must support each conclusion that depends on them.
Heading uncertainty, false aid detections, uncertain chart positions, or map errors require correspondingly wider support

or a revised observation model. The current experiment does
not characterize those cases.
The result establishes that one corrected simulated
Charleston frame can be searched over the declared Eastern
Seaboard domain in a recorded 8.462 s, producing a selected
error of 2.060 m while preserving all unresolved local alternatives. The next technical step is a certified visibility-andgrouping test for remaining conservative graph explanations.
Broader multi-site and field evidence is then needed to characterize accuracy, ambiguity, and runtime across conditions.
The present record provides a concrete method and traceable
reduction in completed-search work without treating a successful sampled fix as proof of unique localization.
